# Landing pages

A reusable guide for planning, writing, building and reviewing a landing page.

Start with what the product or idea does and the problem it solves. Let that
content determine the page, rather than starting with a hero, slogan or
section template.

Follow [Files and prose](../README.md#files-and-prose)
and [ASD-STE-100 writing guidance](../asd-ste-100/README.md)
for language, factual accuracy and removal of filler. Apply this guide to the
requested page; it does not require a particular framework or visual style.

## Establish the idea

Read the brief, product material and any existing page. State the concrete
idea in ordinary language: what it is, what someone does with it, what it
produces or changes, and which problem that addresses. Distinguish an
available product from a proposal. Resolve a material gap with a focused
question; do not invent capabilities to make the page sound complete.

Then identify who needs this page, what they already know, and what they
should understand or do next. Use the requested language, tone and scope.
A reader may need to understand a method or inspect a demo, not buy something.

## Outline the page

Choose the questions the page needs to answer and put them in an order that
builds understanding. Give each section a distinct job. Combine sections that
repeat the same point and retain detail needed for a decision, such as limits,
requirements or terms when relevant.

Use the sections the idea needs. A feature grid, testimonial, pricing block
or repeated call to action is not a default requirement. Keep useful existing
structure when revising a page.

## Develop each section

For each section, make these choices concrete enough to write or build:

- **Purpose:** what the reader should understand or decide here.
- **Text:** draft the heading and actual copy, including the action label and
  destination when an action belongs here. Do not stop at instructions such
  as "write a compelling headline."
- **Visual:** describe what is shown, its labels or data, and the relationship
  or result it makes visible. Choose a screenshot, photograph, diagram,
  example, comparison or interactive demonstration to fit that information.
  Explain how it works with the text; a section can stay text-only when a
  visual adds nothing.
- **Support:** identify the source of a claim or the missing material needed
  to finish it. Keep illustrative data visibly distinct from actual results.
  Do not fabricate screenshots, customer quotes, metrics or endorsements as
  evidence of an existing product.

For example, if the supplied product lets salon customers choose available
appointments, a section can say **"Choose an available time"**, explain that
customers select a service and an open slot, and show a labelled booking
screen with those choices. Use a real screen if available; otherwise label
the proposed view as an illustration. An abstract glowing shape would not
explain that interaction.

Give motion a specific explanatory role, such as showing how selecting a
service changes the available slots. State its trigger and the change the
reader sees. Keep essential content readable without animation and provide a
static or reduced-motion alternative. Choose visual style to suit the
product and the user's direction, without forcing a shared palette, layout
or animation system on unrelated pages.

## Review the whole page

Read the outline, text and visuals together:

- Can the reader identify the product, the problem it addresses and the next
  step? Does each section advance that understanding?
- Is a line interchangeable with a slogan for any product? Replace it with
  supported specifics or remove it. Keep a useful explanation, benefit or
  comparison when it helps the reader decide.
- Does each visual show the promised information accurately? Remove repeated
  explanations and decoration that competes with the content.
- Are necessary conditions or unanswered questions hidden by brevity? Check
  claims against the sources, including claims implied by images or motion.
- If a page was built, check the reading order, narrow-screen layout, action
  destinations and interaction states. Verify that keyboard use and reduced
  motion preserve access to the content.

## Deliver within scope

For a planning request, return the outline with section copy and visual
specifications, plus material gaps. For a copy edit or review, return the
requested revision or findings without forcing a full redesign. When asked
to build, use the plan to implement and check the page; do not add a separate
approval round unless a decision or the project's governance requires it.

Keep plans and review evidence in the requested conversation, issue or pull
request, not as a second source of product knowledge. A new product promise
still follows the [knowledge workflow](../../governances/artifact-driven/workflow.md)
and the project's approval process. This guide does not authorize publishing
or changing that process.

For a page associated with a Flow, follow
[Design links](../../governances/artifact-driven/knowledge.md#design-links)
when connecting its design to the knowledge.
